Educational Use Only: This calculator is intended for classroom instruction and educational practice only. It is not financial, lending, investment, tax, accounting, or legal advice, and it should not be used as a substitute for official lender disclosures or professional guidance.
Purpose: Use this calculator to apply time value of money concepts to compare multiple borrowing options using the same underlying purchase or borrowing amount. It is designed for examples involving vehicle loans, mortgages, student loans, personal loans, and similar installment loans. Each option can be modeled as either a fixed-rate loan or a simplified variable-rate loan with one assumed future rate change. The calculator performs the arithmetic, but you are responsible for comparing the terms and deciding which differences matter.

From TVM Calculation to Borrowing Decision

Connection to Time Value of Money: Once the amount financed is determined, the loan becomes a TVM problem. The amount financed is the PV, the loan payment is the PMT, the annual interest rate is I/Y, the number of monthly payments is N, and a fully amortizing loan generally ends with FV = 0. This calculator extends that TVM setup by adding real-world borrowing features such as down payments, vehicle rebates, fees, different terms, and fixed versus variable rates.

Starting Purchase / Borrowing Amount

Interest Rate vs. APR: The interest rate is the rate used to calculate interest on the loan balance. APR is a broader standardized measure of borrowing cost that may include certain finance charges. This calculator uses the interest rate (I/Y) for payment calculations. Do not automatically enter APR in place of the interest rate unless your problem specifically instructs you to do so.

Loan Options

Add two or more loan options. Each option may have a different rate structure, rate, term, down payment, and fee structure. Enter rates as percentages, such as 6.5 for 6.5%.

Fees: Upfront fees are paid separately and are not added to the loan balance. Financed fees are added to the amount borrowed and therefore may also generate interest. If a quoted APR already incorporates certain fees, adding those same fees again may double-count them.
What should I compare? Look beyond the payment. Compare Payment → Interest → Fees → Total Borrowing Cost → Term → Rate Risk.

How to Use This Calculator

1. Enter the Starting Purchase or Borrowing Amount

Enter the purchase price or borrowing amount before subtracting any down payment or vehicle rebate and before adding financed fees. Using the same starting amount for every option makes the comparison more meaningful.

You may also enter a Borrowing Purpose / Problem Title, such as Compare financing options for a vehicle purchase or Mortgage options for a $250,000 home purchase. This optional label will appear as the heading above the comparison results so you can easily identify the scenario being analyzed.

2. Add the Loan Options

Give each option a descriptive name and choose the type of loan. The calculator will show or hide certain fields when they are especially relevant to that loan type, such as the vehicle rebate field. Enter 0 for fields that do not apply to the problem.

3. Choose Fixed or Variable Rate

For a Fixed Rate, enter the Annual Interest Rate that remains constant for the full term. For a Variable Rate, the field changes to Initial Annual Interest Rate. Enter that starting rate, when you want to assume the rate changes, and the new annual rate you want to model after that change.

The variable-rate option is a simplified classroom model with one assumed rate change. It is useful for illustrating how future rate changes can affect payments and total borrowing cost.

4. Enter the Loan Term

Enter the length of the loan in months or years. The calculator converts years to months and assumes monthly payments.

5. Enter a Down Payment When Applicable

For loans such as vehicle loans or mortgages, enter any cash down payment made before financing. The amount entered in the Down Payment field directly reduces the total amount that must be borrowed. The calculator subtracts the down payment from the starting amount before adding any financed fees.

If the loan does not normally involve a down payment, enter 0.

6. Enter a Vehicle Rebate When Applicable

When Vehicle Loan is selected, a Rebate / Purchase Incentive field appears. Use it for a manufacturer or dealer cash rebate that reduces the vehicle purchase price. The rebate reduces the amount that must be financed, just like a reduction in purchase price.

The rebate field is available only for vehicle-purchase calculations so students do not accidentally apply it to unrelated loan types.

7. Enter Any Fees

Use Upfront Fees for costs paid separately at the beginning of the loan. These fees are not added to the amount borrowed and therefore are not included in the balance used to calculate the loan payment. Use Financed Fees for fees that are added to the loan balance.

8. Compare More Than the Monthly Payment

Review the monthly payment, total interest, total fees, total borrowing cost, and total cash paid. The option with the lowest payment may not be the option with the lowest total cost.

9. Review the TVM Translation

The entry fields and results now show the related TVM variable in parentheses where appropriate. For example, Loan Term (N), Annual Interest Rate (I/Y), Amount Financed (PV), and Monthly Payment (PMT). Each loan's detailed results also include a TVM Translation showing how the borrowing option maps to PV, PMT, I/Y, N, and FV. For fixed-rate loans, the calculator also displays an Excel PMT equivalent and a BA II Plus setup. For variable-rate loans, it shows the two connected TVM phases.

Understanding the Comparison Measures

Down Payment

A down payment is cash paid toward the purchase or borrowing need before the loan is calculated. The amount entered in the Down Payment field reduces the total amount that must be borrowed, which generally lowers the amount financed and the resulting loan payment. It also increases the amount of cash paid at the beginning.

Vehicle Rebate / Purchase Incentive

For a vehicle-purchase option, a rebate or purchase incentive reduces the effective purchase price and therefore reduces the amount that must be financed. It is not treated as a loan payment or as cash paid by the borrower.

Amount Financed (PV)

Amount Financed = Starting Amount − Down Payment − Vehicle Rebate + Financed Fees

For non-vehicle loans, the vehicle rebate is zero. The resulting amount financed is the balance used as PV in the TVM calculation.

Monthly Payment

For a fixed-rate product, this is the scheduled monthly payment for the modeled term. For a variable-rate product, the calculator shows both the initial monthly payment and the recalculated payment after the assumed rate change.

Total Interest

The total scheduled payments minus the amount financed. This represents the interest paid over the full scheduled term if all payments are made as assumed.

Upfront Fees

Upfront fees are costs paid separately rather than financed. They are included when comparing total fees and total cash paid, but they are not added to the amount borrowed for payment calculations.

Total Fees

The sum of upfront fees and financed fees entered for that loan option.

Total Borrowing Cost

Total Borrowing Cost = Total Interest + Upfront Fees + Financed Fees

This is the additional cost associated with obtaining the original amount needed, excluding repayment of the original borrowed amount.

Total Cash Paid

Total Cash Paid = Down Payment + Scheduled Loan Payments + Upfront Fees

This represents the total amount of cash paid toward the purchase or borrowing need over the life of the loan under the assumptions entered.

Fixed Rate Versus Variable Rate

A fixed-rate loan uses the same interest rate for the entire modeled term, so the scheduled principal-and-interest payment remains constant. A variable-rate loan can change over time. In this calculator, you specify one assumed future rate change.

When the rate changes, the calculator estimates the remaining loan balance and recalculates the payment needed to amortize that balance over the remaining term. This allows you to compare how a plausible future rate increase or decrease could affect both the payment and the total cost.

Why Term Matters

Extending the term generally lowers the monthly payment because repayment is spread across more months. However, interest is usually paid for a longer period, which can substantially increase total cost.

Connecting Borrowing Decisions to Time Value of Money

The Loan Is Still a TVM Problem

Every standard installment loan in this calculator is built on the same time value of money concepts you have already learned. The Starting Purchase / Borrowing Amount is not itself a TVM variable. After subtracting any down payment or applicable vehicle rebate and adding any financed fees, the resulting Amount Financed becomes PV. Once that amount is determined, the borrowing problem can be translated directly into the five TVM variables.

Three Levels of the Borrowing Decision

  1. TVM Mechanics: What is the payment?
  2. Borrowing Structure: What amount are we actually financing after down payments, applicable vehicle rebates, and financed fees?
  3. Borrowing Decision: How do payment, interest, fees, term, and rate structure change the total cost?

The calculator does not replace TVM. It applies TVM to a more realistic borrowing decision. A useful way to think about the progression is: Purchase or borrowing details → Amount financed → TVM setup → Payment → Total cost comparison.

Variable-Rate Loans Are Two Connected TVM Problems

In the simplified variable-rate model, the first TVM problem uses the original amount financed and initial interest rate. At the assumed reset point, the remaining loan balance becomes the PV for a second TVM problem. The second problem uses the new assumed interest rate and the remaining number of payments to calculate a new PMT.

Calculation Method & Assumptions

Monthly Payment Formula

For a positive monthly interest rate, the calculator uses the standard fully amortizing installment-loan payment relationship:

Payment = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)−n]

  • P = amount financed
  • r = monthly interest rate
  • n = number of monthly payments

When the stated annual interest rate is zero, payment equals amount financed divided by the number of payments.

Interest Convention

The calculator assumes each stated annual interest rate is divided by 12 to determine the monthly rate. For fixed-rate loans, that rate is used for the full term. For variable-rate loans, the calculator uses the initial rate until the selected reset month, calculates the remaining principal balance, then recalculates the payment using the assumed new rate for the remaining months.

BA II Plus Rounding Differences

Excel and this calculator retain more internal decimal precision than a BA II Plus may display on screen. When the BA II Plus amortization worksheet uses a payment rounded to the calculator's displayed decimal places, the remaining balance and a later recalculated payment may differ by a few cents from this calculator or Excel. A very small difference after an amortization step does not necessarily indicate an error.

For comparable monthly-loan calculations, use P/Y = 12, C/Y = 12, and END mode.

The variable-rate feature is deliberately simplified. It models only one rate change and does not model rate indexes, margins, periodic or lifetime caps, floors, teaser periods, multiple resets, daily simple interest, negative amortization, interest-only periods, or graduated payments.

Mortgage Costs

Property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA charges, maintenance, and other housing expenses are not included unless they are entered as loan fees. Those costs are important to affordability but are not part of the installment-loan payment calculation used here.

Student Loans

The calculator assumes repayment begins immediately under a standard fixed-payment schedule. It does not model deferment, grace periods, income-driven repayment, interest subsidies, capitalization events, forgiveness, or changing payment plans.
